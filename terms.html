2. What Alkadiet is — and what it is not

Alkadiet estimates the acid or base load your food leaves for your kidneys to clear, using PRAL (Potential Renal Acid Load), a formula published by Remer & Manz in 1995 and validated against urine measurements. You log what you eat; the app computes a daily figure and shows it on a colour scale.

What it is not:

  • Not a medical device. Alkadiet does not diagnose, treat, prevent, monitor or predict any disease or condition, and is not intended to. It is a food tracker with a scientific scoring metric.
  • Not medical, dietary or nutritional advice. Nothing in the app is a recommendation to eat, avoid, start or stop anything. Talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have kidney disease, diabetes, an eating disorder, are pregnant, or take prescribed medication.
  • Not a health score. PRAL only sees protein and minerals. Fats and sugars score near zero because the formula cannot see them — that makes butter neutral on this scale, not healthy. A green score is not a verdict that a food is good for you.
  • Not a pH value. PRAL is a load measured in mEq. It cannot be converted into a pH, and the app therefore never shows one. Anyone who tells you a banana "has pH 7.8" is making it up.

You are responsible for what you eat. Use Alkadiet as one honest signal about your diet, not as a verdict on it.

6. The AI photo scan

When you photograph a meal, the image is sent through our relay to a third-party AI vision model (Google Gemini) which returns guesses at what is on the plate and how much it weighs. See the privacy policy §5.1 and §11.

You must understand and accept how rough this is:

  • The output is an estimate produced by a statistical model. It is often wrong. It will miss hidden ingredients — oil, butter, dressing, sauce, sugar — because it cannot see them.
  • PRAL is very sensitive to protein. A misjudged portion of cheese or meat moves your score materially. The app is built so you can correct every item and every gram weight, and it marks low-confidence items. Correcting them is your job, not the model's.
  • Never rely on a scan you have not checked, and never rely on it for a decision that matters to your health.
  • The scan needs a connection and is limited (currently 20 scans per device per day, and a per-minute limit at the relay). Manual search works offline and is always the more accurate route.

7. Food data

  • Nutrient values in the bundled database are derived from USDA FoodData Central, a US government dataset in the public domain. We derive PRAL from those values.
  • Nutrient databases contain errors, averages and gaps, and the food in front of you is not the average of its category. Values in the app are estimates. We do not warrant that any figure matches what you are eating.
